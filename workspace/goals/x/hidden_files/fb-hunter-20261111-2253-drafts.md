Good question, and it's one of the least talked-about parts — a face and neck lift is usually done under general anesthesia, or deep IV sedation with local, and the protocol really does vary by surgeon. Doing the neck at the same time as the face is actually the way to keep it to one anesthetic instead of two, which fits what you said about minimizing times under. The two things I'd ask are whether a board-certified anesthesiologist is present the whole time, and how long they expect you to be under with the fat transfer added. Is your bigger worry the total time under, or who's watching you while you're under?

The honest answer is it depends on what's making the lid look heavy — CO2 laser resurfaces and tightens the skin itself, so it can smooth crepey lid skin, but it can't remove excess loose skin that's folding over. Is the crepey texture what bothers you most, or the heaviness you notice by the end of the day?
