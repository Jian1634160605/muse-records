## E1 · 顶楼回复"5 年前拉皮、下颌缘又松了，想做 mini、求便宜医生"
post=1430573648590738（Facelift Support Group，19:47 新帖，0 评论）
策略：二次拉皮决策框架——mini 为什么通常修不好复发性 jowls、瘢痕下 SMAS、便宜报价风险、两次面诊比诚实度

---
Five years after a first lift, recurrent jowls usually mean the SMAS layer has re-stretched — a 'mini' (mostly skin tightening through short scars) rarely fixes that. And on a previously operated face there's scar tissue and less tissue left to work with, so a revision is technically harder, not easier. That's exactly why the cheapest quote is the riskiest place to save: a surgeon who does a high volume of secondary lifts will tell you honestly what a second operation can and can't improve — usually a better jawline and neck, not a reset to year one. In consults ask: how many secondary facelifts do you do per year, what's your plan for the SMAS under the old scar tissue, and what's your revision policy in writing. Get two consults and compare how honest the answers feel — that comparison is free, and it's worth more than the price difference.

## E2 · 顶楼回复"韩国 hidden stitch 拉皮、和泰国/巴厘岛比价"
post=1430575815257188（Facelift Support Group，19:50 新帖，0 评论）
策略：拆 "hidden stitch" 营销话术——切口藏法 vs 皮下层面才是关键；all-in 比价框架；飞去做手术的 follow-up 物流

---
'Hidden stitch' is mostly marketing language — every good facelift hides its incisions in the hairline and around the ear. The real question isn't the stitch, it's what happens under the skin: a skin-only or thread-based 'mini' lift vs SMAS/deep-plane work. Short-scar techniques suit mild laxity; real jowling and neck laxity need SMAS-level work no matter which country it's done in. On cost: Korea, Thailand and Bali all have huge ranges, and the number that matters is the all-in price — anesthesia, facility fee, post-op visits, and the revision policy in writing — not the headline quote. Two things people forget when flying for surgery: (1) ask for that surgeon's own patients at 12+ months, unfiltered, with a similar aging pattern — one-month photos flatter every technique; (2) ask who manages you at week 3 when you're home and something looks off. The cheapest ticket can become the most expensive complication.
