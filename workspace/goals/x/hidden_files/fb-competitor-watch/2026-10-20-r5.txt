R1 draft (reply, parent 2256421944923742, post 1735683088565927 Nayak Lisa-64):
Totally normal worry — and in most cases, yes, it goes away. What you're seeing is usually residual swelling plus skin that hasn't finished redraping yet. The neck is the slowest area to settle: some wrinkling or crepey texture in the first weeks is common, and it typically smooths over 6 to 12 weeks as swelling resolves. The honest milestone surgeons use is 6 months for the neck to show its real contour. Two things worth knowing: if the platysma muscle was tightened, early tightness can exaggerate the wrinkled look before it relaxes; and at a consult, ask how much skin is actually excised versus just redraped, because real laxity needs excision, not only tightening. If wrinkles are still prominent at 3 months, that's the right time to ask your surgeon directly — not week 2.

R2 draft (top-level, post 1429347325380037 Facelift Support Group, Turkey pricing/deep plane):
Great instinct — that missing "deep plane" wording is exactly the detail that separates comparable quotes. A few things worth adding to your list, from the surgical side: (1) "deep plane" should be specified for the face AND the neck separately. Some quotes do a deep plane face but only liposuction or skin-only work on the neck, and the neck is what ages the result fastest. (2) Ask whether the quote is itemized: surgeon's fee, anesthesia, facility, overnight stay, garments and meds — a headline number hides a lot. (3) Ask about the revision policy in writing: what happens, and what it costs, if you need a touch-up within the first year. (4) Be cautious of quotes bundling chin or lip add-ons you didn't ask for — that's often margin, not medicine. Your comparison list is a smart approach; the surgeons who answer these questions precisely are usually the ones worth shortlisting.

R3 draft (top-level, post 1429383562043080 Facelift Support Group, where/what to lift):
Good instinct hunting for unfiltered photos — that is exactly how you should judge. One framework that helps: under-eyes and jaw sagging are usually different problems with different fixes. A facelift done well lifts the jawline and neck, but it doesn't directly fix under-eye bags or hollowing — that's typically lower blepharoplasty and/or fat transfer, a separate decision. So at a consult, ask the surgeon to map each concern to a specific procedure instead of selling one operation for everything. Three questions that separate careful surgeons: (1) can I see your patients at 12+ months, unfiltered, in similar age and skin type; (2) what exactly will you do for the under-eye area versus the jawline — two answers, not one; (3) what is your revision rate and policy. Being specific about what bothers you actually makes you easier to plan for — bring those same photos to the consult.
